# Abstract

The development of general language-guided robotic systems capable of manipulating in unstructured environments is a significant challenge. While progress in large language models (LLMs) enables robots to integrate extensive world knowledge, they lack 3D spatial understanding required for precise manipulation tasks, fail to seamlessly align rich semantic reconstruction with long-horizon manipulation. To address these challenges, we present 3D-Planner, an end-to-end framework that generates collision-free trajectories from human instructions and semantic reconstruction. 3D-Planner incorporates language-conditioned features into a NeRF-inspired 3D point cloud to realize the integrated perception of geometric information and semantic information. In this context, we conduct high-level planning through iterative feedback and lifelong memory, decomposes natural language goals into sequenced sub-tasks. By extracting actionable 3D spatial constraints from perception and planning, 3D-Planner generates a dense sequence of collision-free 6-DoF end-effector trajectories. In hardware experiments on a ROKAE robot, 3D-Planner achieves a 78.8% success rate in low-level tasks (compared to 60% for baselines) and improves high-level planning accuracy by 15.1% while requiring 1.3 fewer steps on average. In addition, 3D-Planner raise multi-stage manipulation success by 21.7% compared to Cap and Voxposer. Demo videos and an overview of 3D-Planner are available at https://dajiebi.github.io/3D-Planner.github.io/.
